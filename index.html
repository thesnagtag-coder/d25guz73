Before you start - safety first

HSE
If you smell gas: get fresh air and open doors and windows; turn off the gas emergency control valve at the meter (unless it is in a basement or cellar); put out naked flames and don't smoke; don't operate electrical switches; call your local gas emergency number. (Gas Safe Register)
If a CO alarm sounds: leave the home immediately and call your local emergency number. Seek prompt medical attention if anyone feels dizzy, light-headed or sick. (CDC Carbon Monoxide)
  • Look only. Never remove covers, adjust controls, relight a pilot you are unsure about, or block or unblock a flue yourself. (Professional practice)
  • Only use a registered, qualified gas engineer or technician. In the UK this means Gas Safe registered. (Gas Safe Register)
  • Pre-Purchase: ask before switching any appliance on. (Professional practice)

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It does not test for gas leaks or measure carbon monoxide. Only alarms and qualified engineers with the right instruments can do that.
    • It never asks you to open, adjust, relight or repair a fuel-burning appliance, flue or meter.
    • It does not replace a gas safety check or landlord certificate required by law in your country.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • Signs of an unsafe gas appliance: a lazy yellow flame; the pilot light keeps going out; black marks or stains on or around the appliance; increased condensation inside windows. (Gas Safe Register, Cadent Gas)
    • CO symptoms: headaches, dizziness, breathlessness, nausea, collapse and loss of consciousness. (Gas Safe Register)
    • Yearly servicing: heating system, water heater and any gas, oil or coal burning appliances serviced by a qualified technician every year; all gas appliances serviced and safety checked every year (UK). (CDC Carbon Monoxide, Gas Safe Register)
    • CO alarms: install a battery-operated or battery back-up CO detector; check or replace the battery each spring and autumn. Fit an audible CO alarm as a second line of defence (UK). (CDC Carbon Monoxide, Gas Safe Register)
    • Never: heat your house with a gas oven; run a car or truck inside an attached garage, even with the door open; burn anything in a stove or fireplace that is not vented; use a generator, charcoal grill, camp stove or other gasoline- or charcoal-burning device inside the home, basement or garage, or outside less than 6 m (20 ft) from a window, door or vent. (CDC Carbon Monoxide)
    • If you smell gas: fresh air, open doors and windows; turn off the emergency control valve at the meter (unless in a basement or cellar); no naked flames or smoking; don't operate electrical switches; call the gas emergency number; don't turn the gas back on until checked by a registered engineer. (Gas Safe Register)
    • Chimneys: have heating equipment and chimneys cleaned and inspected each year by a professional. (USFA Heating)
    • Units: 1 m = 3.28 ft. Metric first, imperial in brackets.
    Country-specific: gas engineer registration schemes, landlord gas safety duties, CO alarm laws and emergency numbers differ by country. "Gas Safe" is the UK register. Check your local gas supplier and regulator.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Rules where you live

      The advice in this tool applies everywhere. These notes cover rules that differ between countries. Rules change, so always confirm with your local authority or a registered professional.

      • Emergency numbers: South Africa: 10111 (police), 10177 (ambulance and fire), 112 from a mobile phone. Saudi Arabia: 911 or 999 (police), 997 (ambulance), 998 (civil defence and fire). UAE: 999 (police), 998 (ambulance), 997 (civil defence and fire). UK: 999 or 112. US and Canada: 911. Australia: 000. Save your local number in your phone today.
        Source: Gauteng Tourism - Emergency contacts (South Africa) · Expatica - Emergency numbers in Saudi Arabia (updated Apr 2025) · UAE Government portal - Handling emergencies
      • South Africa: gas Certificate of Conformity: Gas installations, such as gas stoves, heaters and gas geysers, need a Certificate of Conformity from an installer registered with LPGAS SA. When a home is sold, the seller must give the buyer a copy (Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009). Confirm the details with a registered gas installer.

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
